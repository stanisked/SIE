# Live steering v2 floor trial and v3 envelope candidate

Recorded 2026-10-28. The operator reported approximately 0.19 m of floor travel and visible left steering during a requested 0.20 m live-steered command. Evidence: `/home/elwis/.local/state/sie/evidence/v7_live_steered_forward_1793179509_5b4d5d8f/summary.json` and `http.jsonl`, copied by the operator.

The v2 runner recorded one accepted `/move-forward-steered`, two accepted `/steer-update` calls at −7 and −4 PWM while `DRIVING`, and one accepted `/stop`. The negative bias boosted the right wheel, matching the observed left turn. The command ended `FAULT` with `BOUNDED_DISTANCE_LIMIT`, then status confirmed zero PWM, no active command and complete remote-stop observation. Encoder-estimated final travel was right 0.2049 m, left 0.1770 m, center 0.1910 m; heading was 0.0963 rad (about 5.5 degrees). The floor-distance report is independent operator evidence, not a centimeter-accurate calibration.

The v2 controller applied a straight-line per-wheel cap near 0.204 m to a curved drive. The outer wheel reached that cap while the center remained under 0.20 m. A `GET /status` started at host monotonic 9713.528 s and timed out after 0.463 s; `/stop` was accepted immediately afterward. The firmware's `loop()` calls `server.handleClient()` before `updateMotion()`. The installed Arduino WebServer can wait for a response inside that call. The journal does not prove exactly where the delay occurred, but it reveals a scheduling risk for motor control during HTTP latency.

V3 changes only the steered-forward endpoint's path envelope: requested distance refers to center arc travel; normal braking uses center progress; settled completion for 0.20 m is 0.18–0.22 m; hard caps are requested center +0.02 m, each wheel requested center +0.075 m, and |heading| <0.35 rad. The wheel allowance exceeds the combination of maximum permitted center overrun and half-track yaw travel. Earlier v2 travel falls inside this new envelope. Old `/move-forward` and turn endpoints keep their previous bounds.

A separate higher-priority ESP32 task checks the steered command's wheel/center/heading hard caps and a 300 ms control-loop service deadline. It latches an electrical brake even if WebServer handling stalls; the main loop then records the fault. The Pi runner avoids a redundant status request before every frame after `DRIVING` is confirmed, while retaining fresh-frame checks, command-bound steer updates and terminal stop confirmation.

V3 compiled for ESP32 core 3.3.12; eleven dry-run Pi tests and Python compilation passed. Concurrency and braking behavior require one raised-wheel test before a floor test. No v3 motor trial has been performed. Canonical Measurement and autonomous motion admission remain unchanged.
